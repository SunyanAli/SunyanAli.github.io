The three things that actually break

  1. It talks over people

    Barge-in and endpointing are tuned wrong, so the agent interrupts, or leaves two seconds of dead air and the caller says "hello?" into silence. Most of the fix is in turn detection, not in the prompt.

  2. It confirms bookings that never happened

    The classic one. The agent checks availability, then writes the appointment as a second step — and in the gap between the two, the slot goes. The write itself has to be the reservation, with the calendar rejecting the conflict. Otherwise you find out from the customer standing at your door.

  3. It's built as if the law doesn't apply

    Since 2 August 2026, EU AI Act Article 50(1) requires the caller be told they're speaking to an AI from the start of the first interaction. In the US, the FCC's 2024 ruling makes AI-generated voices "artificial" under the TCPA — $500 a call, more if wilful. These are first-sentence and consent-architecture decisions. Retrofitting them is ugly.
